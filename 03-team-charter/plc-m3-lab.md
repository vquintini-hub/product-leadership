# Set the Habits That Make a Team Faster, Module 3 Lab

## Name the situation
- **Who they are (role, not name), what you have observed, and how long it has been happening.:** The founder of a client company I advise. He repeatedly tells me the product team does not understand the market and does not do what he wants. He makes the product strategy and product decisions himself, and the team receives features and sprints to deliver. I have not seen him set explicit expectations with the team, show them what good looks like, or tell them what is missing in what they deliver. This has been going on for six months to a year, since I started working with them.

## Make a diagnosis
- **Your diagnosis, plus one sentence on why. Is your frustration with their behavior, or with a decision you made?:** Clarity problem. The team has never been told what good looks like or which decisions are theirs, because the founder keeps strategy and decisions to himself. They are operating without a standard, not failing to meet one. My frustration is with his behaviour, and also with my own choice not to name the pattern to him sooner.

## Write your opening line
- **One next action I will take in the next two weeks is:** Within two weeks, book a dedicated 45-minute conversation with the founder, separate from our regular agenda, to talk only about how he and the product team work together.
- **The first sentence of the conversation I need to have is:** "In our last check-in, when you said the team does not understand the market, I noticed we have never written down what you expect from them or which decisions are theirs, and that gap is costing you their judgment. Can we look at that together?"

## AI role-play
- **After you step out: what did the role-play change about how you will open this conversation for real?:** My opening landed as an instruction ("you need to tell them"), and with no specific moment in it he defended his expertise instead of looking at the gap. For the real conversation I will start from a specific recent moment, name his market knowledge as the strength it is, and ask what it would take for the team to bring him an insight he had not seen. Then I will offer the choice as a question: does he want them to execute the story, or own part of it?

## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** Retention: keeping people on Fable once they feel better. We own the app experience for paying users. PM 1: the first 90 days. PM 2: life after day 90, including the record of staying well. PM 3: the weekly check-in and its reminders. We are measured on KR1 to KR3.
- **What is out of scope.:** Bringing new users in and turning them into paying users: owned by the Growth team. It matters, and we work with them where our areas meet. Prices: a leadership decision. Clinical care and crisis support: outside what Fable offers.
- **Cross-boundary decisions that need a joint call.:** If a change touches another PM's area, both agree before it ships. The main case is the weekly check-in (PM 3), which appears in PM 1's and PM 2's areas. Upgrade messages in the app need Growth's agreement too.
- **How We Decide. Who decides feature and scope calls.:** Each PM decides alone in their own area. In a shared area, the two PMs decide together. If they cannot agree, I decide, based on what helps people keep Fable once they are well.
- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** If two PMs disagree for more than two days, they come to me together on a call, not on Slack. I decide within one day.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** I handle disagreements with other teams, such as Growth or leadership. I answer within two days and write the decision down.

## Show and swap your team charter
- **Where does the charter leave room for interpretation that could cause a conflict?:** Clear overall. One open point: who decides how many reminders a person gets in a week? PM 2 and PM 3 could each add one, and users would feel pushed.
