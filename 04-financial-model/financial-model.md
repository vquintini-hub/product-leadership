> **The case in one paragraph:** The bet Many people pay for Fable through a hard time and cancel once they feel better. I want to give them a reason to stay: a weekly wellness record that shows how they have been doing, built from their practice and sleep data. If they cancel, they lose it. More people stay on premium, so revenue goes up (KR3). What I am assuming (none of these numbers are in the brief) 1. The wellness record raises the share of people still paying at day 180 from 25% to 40%. This matters most, so I will test it: a small group will not get the wellness record, and the difference between the two groups shows what it really does. 2. 20,000 paying users reach day 90 every quarter. 3. Premium costs $9.99 a month. 4. People still paying at day 180 stay about six more months. 5. The first version costs $200,000 to build and $50,000 a year to run. What it returns Each extra person who keeps paying is worth about $60 (six months at $9.99). To cover the first-year cost of $250,000, we need about 4,200 extra paying users a year ($250,000 / $60), which is about 1,050 every quarter. Each quarter 20,000 users reach day 90, and 1,050 of them is about 5%. So the share still paying at day 180 must rise by at least 5 percentage points, from 25% to 30%, just to break even. My target is 15 points, from 25% to 40%: 3,000 extra paying users a quarter, about 12,000 and $720,000 in the first year, almost three times the cost. We see the first sign at day 120. When I stop On 31 March 2027 I compare the two groups at day 120. I stop if the share still paying in the group with the wellness record is less than 6 percentage points higher than in the group without it (for example, 30% against 25% is only 5 points, so we would stop), or if more than 17% (1 in 6) of the people we kept cancel each month. Either one means the wellness record will not pay for itself. Then we do not fund the next phase (full Apple Health and Oura), and the engineers move to the onboarding redesign in Q2 2027. We do not wait another quarter.

## 2. Kill criteria

> **Kill criterion:** On 31 March 2027 we compare premium retention at day 120 between users with the wellness record and a group without it. If the gap is less than 6 points, or if more than 1 in 6 kept users cancel each month, the wellness record has not shown it can pay back its $250,000 first-year cost. We stop building it, do not fund the next phase (full Apple Health and Oura), and move its engineers to the onboarding redesign in Q2 2027. We do not wait another quarter.

## Lab notes

**Make your evaluation and funding decision**

- **What assumption is doing the most work? If this number is 20-30% off, what changes?:** The 4% individual-to-team conversion. At 2.8% to 3.2%, converting users fall from 120 to 84 to 96 a quarter and Q1 ARR from $103,680 to about $73,000 to $83,000. CAC per converting user rises from about $950 to $1,190 to $1,360, so payback stretches to 16 to 19 months.
- **What is the structural problem in this case? Look past the headline numbers for something that does not hold up on closer inspection.:** The payback is wrong. CAC is $38 per individual, but only 4% convert, so CAC per converting user is about $950 against $72 a month in team revenue: about 13 months, not 4.7. The 15% team churn is never used, and repeating 120 conversions every quarter assumes the pool of 3,000 weekly-actives refills. For Fable, a team tier is a B2B product with new buyers, outside the existing freemium and premium model.
- **Is the kill criterion complete and actionable? Does it name the consequence, or hand the decision back to the room?:** No. It names the metric, a threshold and a date, but "the go-to-market approach is reassessed" is not a consequence: it hands the decision back to the room. And the 3% threshold sits below the 4% the case needs, so the bet could miss its own return and still survive.
- **Your verdict: FUND / FUND WITH ONE CONDITION / DO NOT FUND. If a condition, name it; otherwise explain in one sentence.:** DO NOT FUND. The corrected payback is about 13 months, the case ignores churn, and a team tier is the Fable for Teams bet I ruled out as a Hard No. What would change my mind: a pilot showing at least 4% conversion with payback under 12 months on fully loaded CAC, without pulling engineers from the post-day-90 work.
